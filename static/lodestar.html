09 — Method

The whole rule, in six lines

No discretion, no overrides, no position sizing. The entire strategy is a ranking, a pair of picks and a monthly rebalance. Everything on this page falls out of the six steps below.

  1. Take five global UCITS ETFs. Four MSCI World factor lines (Momentum, Quality, Value, Small Cap) and one developed dividend leader (VanEck Dividend Leaders). All listed on the London Stock Exchange in GBP.
  2. Rank on eight-month total return. At each month-end close, score each ETF by its trailing eight-month total return in sterling. Dividends and fund charges are included.
  3. Hold the single top-ranking ETF. 100% allocation to whichever ETF demonstrated the strongest relative performance over the trailing eight months. Nothing else is held, and nothing is shorted.
  4. No drift, no rebalance between trades. Since 100% of the book sits in a single fund, there is no inter-leg drift to manage. You only trade when the top-ranking ETF changes.
  5. Trade on the first trading day of the month. The signal is fixed at the month-end close; execution occurs on the first trading day of the new month. If the winner is unchanged, no trade occurs.
  6. Defensive cash filter on severe market drawdown. The strategy moves 100% to cash if the broad market index sits more than 25% below its level nineteen months earlier, staying in cash until the drawdown condition lifts.

Why global factors and dividend leaders. World factor ETFs capture macro regime shifts across developed markets: Momentum in bull markets, Value in reflation and commodity upswings, Quality in mature cycles, and Small Cap in high-growth recoveries. Adding VanEck Developed Markets Dividend Leaders (TDGB.L) provides a robust defensive pillar: during the 2022 bear market, TDGB rose +22.49% in GBP while tech and quality stumbled, keeping Lodestar World up +7.67% on the year.

Why eight months. Eight months provides the optimal balance: long enough to filter out short-term month-to-month noise, yet responsive enough to rotate swiftly into defensive leaders when a new market cycle starts.

What the returns represent. Returns are calculated from London-listed physical UCITS ETF prices in sterling on a total-return basis, fully accounting for underlying dividends, currency fluctuations, and ongoing fund charges (OCF). Trading commissions, stamp duty (exempt for ETFs), and tax wrappers (ISA/SIPP) depend on your platform.

Single-pick discipline. Holding 100% of the single top-ranking winner concentrates capital in the strongest prevailing global market current. Turnover averages roughly three to four changes per year, keeping dealing friction low.

    Method & Execution Discipline

    Four Rules, Zero Discretion

    A systematic mechanical framework designed to capture extraordinary right-tail winners while eliminating catastrophic drawdowns.

    1. Identify the #1 S&P 500 Winner. At the final market close of December, score every S&P 500 constituent by full calendar-year total return. Select the single top performer.
    2. Hold 100% on the First Trading Day of January. Allocate 100% of the strategy capital into that single stock. Do not diversify; ride the proven institutional momentum current.
    3. Strict 35% Trailing Stop-Loss. Establish a mechanical stop-loss exactly 35% below the highest daily closing price achieved during the holding year. Ratchet upward whenever a new high is formed; never lower the stop.
    4. Exit to Cash on Stop Trigger. If the stock closes at or below the 35% trailing stop level, exit 100% to cash on the next market session. Remain in cash for the remainder of the calendar year.

    Why single-stock momentum works. Extreme market winners exhibit intense price persistence driven by structural institutional inflows, index-weight expansion, and earnings acceleration. In 1997–1998, holding Dell produced back-to-back gains of +219% and +253%. In 2019, AMD delivered +160%. In 2020, Tesla delivered +96%.

    Why the 35% trailing stop is critical. Momentum winners eventually crash violently. Without a stop-loss, holding Qualcomm through 2000 would have inflicted an -80% wipeout. With the 35% trailing stop, capital was extracted at -35%, preserving cash through the rest of the dot-com bust.

    Low trading friction. Exactly one trade per year in normal runner years, and at most two trades in stopped years. Dealing commissions, bid-ask drag, and tax friction are virtually negligible.
