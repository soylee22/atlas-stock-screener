DATA COVERAGE

Data & methodology

Coverage and refresh

Listings come from Yahoo’s regional equity screener. A listing market can differ from a company’s home country. Multiple share classes and depositary receipts remain separate rows. Stocks are shown by default. Yahoo also returns some ETFs, warrants and other instruments. The default view excludes recognised non-stock instruments.

Main listings only

This switch hides OTC quotes, London secondary venues and recognised depositary wrappers, funds and leveraged products. Matching company names are compared across all six markets before your filters run. A home-market listing is preferred when Yahoo country data is available. Otherwise, the busiest recognised exchange listing is retained. Separate share classes in that market remain visible. This is a practical listing filter, rather than an exchange-certified primary-listing register. Switch it off to see every listing.

Quotes and currency rates refresh once a day while the local service runs. Use Refresh data for a new universe scan. Financials load in a rolling queue, prioritising visible rows, and are cached for seven days. Blank metrics mean unavailable or awaiting retrieval.

Company icons

Icons use the company website reported by Yahoo and are fetched through Google’s favicon service. Files are stored locally and reused across listings and share classes. These are website icons and may differ from the logos used by TradingView.

USD conversion

Prices use their quote currency. UK pence prices are converted to pounds first. Market cap uses the base quote currency. Financial statements use their reporting currency. All monetary values use the latest cached Yahoo FX rate, including historical financial totals. Percentages and share volumes retain their original units.

Financial periods

Income and cash flow use four consecutive reported quarters where available, with the latest fiscal year as a fallback. Net margin uses income and revenue for the same period. Capex is a positive expenditure. Free cash flow equals operating cash flow less capex. FCF growth compares the latest two fiscal years and is blank when the previous FCF is non-positive.

Dividend growth

The growth streak counts consecutive increases in total annual dividends per share, ending in the latest completed calendar year. The 5Y rate is the annualised change between six completed calendar-year totals. Calculations use local-currency dividends, so FX movements do not create dividend growth. Yahoo history can be incomplete and can include special dividends. The streak is an observed history, rather than a certified company record.
